💡 How to follow: Conor Harris: a pelvis stuck in anterior pelvic tilt can make the lower belly pooch out. Posture is a two-way street — pooch-belly people usually have BOTH a forward pelvis AND rounded shoulders / collapsed front rib cage. The fix: rotate the pelvis back AND open the chest. This chair-assisted drill does both: lie on your back with feet on a chair (90/90), rest hands on your forehead to open the chest, drag the heels into the chair to tilt the pelvis posteriorly, then breathe — exhale gently through an open mouth until the side abs engage, then inhale through the nose. 2 sets of 10 slow breaths.
1

Why the Belly Pooches

Concept
Understand

A pelvis stuck in anterior pelvic tilt can make the lower belly pooch out — but posture is a two-way street. If the pelvis tips forward, the body balances by rounding the shoulders and collapsing the front rib cage. Fix both, not just the pelvis.

  1. APT pelvis → lower belly pooches
  2. Body compensates: shoulders round, rib cage collapses
  3. Pooch = pelvis forward + chest closed
  4. Fix = rotate pelvis back + open chest
2

Setup — 90/90 on a Chair

Setup
Once

Lie on your back with your feet up on a chair, knees and hips at roughly 90 degrees (a little further away is fine). Tilt the chin passively toward the ceiling and rest your hands on your forehead — no arm tension, chest open.

  1. Feet on chair, knees & hips ~90°
  2. Chin tilts passively toward ceiling
  3. Hands rest on forehead, arms relaxed
  4. Chest in an open position
3

Drag Heels → Posterior Tilt

Action
Gentle, ~2/10

Drag down into the chair with the back of your heels. Hamstrings engage, the pelvis moves into a posterior tilt, and the low back flattens. Very gentle — about 2 out of 10 effort.

  1. Drag heels down into the chair
  2. Hamstrings engage
  3. Pelvis rolls into posterior tilt
  4. Low back flattens — 2/10 effort
4

Breath — 2×10 Slow

Breathe
2 sets × 10 breaths

Exhale gently through an open mouth with a relaxed jaw for 5–10 seconds until the side abs engage. Pause, hold them gently, then inhale through the nose, letting the air fall in. Slow and passive — the front of the rib cage opens more each breath, no neck engagement.

  1. Exhale open-mouth 5–10s, jaw relaxed
  2. Feel the side abs engage at the end
  3. Pause, then inhale through nose
  4. Rib cage opens in front — no neck tension
  5. Repeat: 2 sets × 10 slow breaths
