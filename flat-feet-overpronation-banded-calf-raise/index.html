💡 How to follow: 💡 The idea: a pronating foot collapses inward — the ankle rolls in and the arch drops. A light band wrapped around the heel and anchored under the other foot pulls the heel the opposite way (inside → outward), so every calf raise has to hold the foot in inversion / supination. You're training the position, not stretching. Programming: 3 rounds × 12–15 per side, 1–2 s up / 2–3 s down, barefoot, 3–4× per week. Warm up with card 7, then run cards 2–6 as one continuous set per side. Progress by stepping wider (more tension), then by going single-leg, then by a heavier band. Feel rules: work on the outside of the ankle and under the arch. Sharp pain, tingling, or anything running up the calf or into the foot = stop and reassess. Barefoot only — cushioning hides the collapse.
1

Why the Band Works

Concept
Not specified in reel

The band runs around the heel and pulls it from the inside outward . That single pull forces the hindfoot into inversion/supination — the exact position a flat, over-pronating foot never finds on its own. You're not stretching anything; you're training the arch to hold a position it has been collapsing out of all day.

  1. Over-pronation = the ankle rolls inward and the arch drops with every step
  2. The band pulls the heel the opposite way, from inside → outward
  3. Your job during the drill is simply to not let the foot win
  4. Same idea works barefoot on any floor — no gym needed
  5. Band tension does the coaching: more tension = harder to collapse
2

Wrap the Band Around the Heel

Setup
Not specified in reel

Loop a light resistance band around the heel bone , then bring it over the top of the foot so it sits across the instep. This is the wrap that puts the pull on the right side of the joint.

  1. Barefoot. Sit on the floor with the band looped in front of you
  2. Bring the band around the back of the heel bone first — not the ankle
  3. Run it over the top of the foot, across the instep
  4. Keep the band flat, not twisted, so tension runs cleanly
  5. A light band is plenty — this is a position drill, not a strength max
3

Create Tension Under the Far Foot

Setup
Not specified in reel

Stand up and anchor the other end of the band under the ball of the opposite foot . Step the feet apart to dial in tension — the band should be pulling the working heel outward before you move at all.

  1. Anchor the band under the ball of the other foot
  2. Step wider to increase tension, closer to reduce it
  3. Stand tall, feet hip width, working foot flat
  4. Pre-tension before rep one so the foot starts in the right place
  5. Both feet stay on the floor the whole set
4

Offset Calf Raise (Resisted)

The Drill
Not specified in reel

The main event: with the band live, rise onto the balls of your feet and lower slowly. The offset stance means the feet are staggered — the band pulls one heel outward the entire time, so the calf raise is performed resisting that pull instead of collapsing into it.

  1. Staggered stance — one foot slightly forward, both grounded
  2. Rise onto the balls of the feet, 1–2 s up
  3. Lower slowly, 2–3 s down, no bouncing
  4. Keep resisting the band on the way up and on the way down
  5. 12–15 reps per side. The working side is the one the band pulls outward
5

The Two Cues That Make It Work

Cues
Not specified in reel

Two checks decide whether this drill does anything: pressure on the first metatarsal (the ball of the big toe) stays on the floor, and the toes stay splayed . Miss them and the foot rolls onto its outer edge — the compensation you're trying to train out.

  1. Keep constant pressure under the ball of the big toe (first metatarsal)
  2. If that pressure goes, you roll onto the side of the foot — reset
  3. Toes splayed wide, not gripping or curling
  4. Big toe stays down even when the heel lifts
  5. Slow down until you can feel the ball of the foot the whole rep
6

Hindfoot Inversion at the Top

Finish
Not specified in reel

The payoff position: at the top of the calf raise the ankle rolls outward into hindfoot inversion / supination while the toes stay splayed flat. This is the arch-supporting position — the whole point of the drill, and the last thing to lock in.

  1. At the top, actively roll the ankle outward against the band
  2. Toes stay splayed and flat — no gripping the floor
  3. Hold the top 1 s before lowering
  4. You should feel the work on the outside of the ankle and under the arch
  5. Both sides equal reps — the flatter foot usually sets the pace

Banded Big-Toe Abduction

Support
Not specified in reel

Support move from the verified GIF library. A small loop band around both big toes, pulling them apart while you hold the arch — trains the muscle that abducts the big toe, which is exactly what fails when the arch collapses.

  1. Sit with knees bent, feet flat, small loop band around both big toes
  2. Push the big toes outward against the band, slowly
  3. Hold 2–3 s, then release slowly — 10–12 reps
  4. Keep the heel and ball of the foot down; the movement is in the big toe only
  5. Pairs well before the calf raise as an arch warm-up
