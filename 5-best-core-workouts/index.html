1

Forearm Plank

Anti-extension · rectus + TVA + obliques
3 sets · 20–60s hold

The foundational anti-extension brace — builds stiffness head to heel.

  1. Elbows directly under shoulders, forearms parallel, legs extended
  2. One straight line head→heels — squeeze glutes + quads, no sag or pike
  3. Brace the abs; actively pull elbows toward the toes
  4. Breathe shallow and steady through the hold
2

Side Plank

Anti-lateral flexion · obliques + QL
3 sets · 20–45s / side

Side-lying brace that hits the obliques and quadratus lumborum — the spine's side stabilizers.

  1. Elbow directly under shoulder, forearm down
  2. Stack feet (or bend knees for the easier level), lift hips to a straight line
  3. Ribs down, don't let the hips sag or the shoulder creep to the ear
  4. Hold each side, breathe normally
3

Bird Dog

Anti-rotation · multifidus + erectors
3 sets · 8–10 / side · 2s pause

Contralateral reach that trains the posterior core against rotation and extension.

  1. All fours — knees under hips, hands under shoulders
  2. Extend opposite arm and leg together to parallel with the floor
  3. Keep hips level — no rotating or arching the low back
  4. Pause 2s at full extension, return under control
4

Dead Bug

Anti-extension + coordination · deep TVA
3 sets · 8–12 / side

Limb movement over a locked neutral spine — the gold standard for deep-core motor control.

  1. Lie on your back, arms straight up, knees bent 90° (tabletop)
  2. Lower one arm and the opposite leg toward the floor together
  3. Keep the lower back pressed into the mat the whole time — no arch
  4. Exhale as you lower, return slow, alternate sides
5

McGill Curl-Up

Spine-sparing flexion · rectus isolation
3 sets · 6–8 × 8–10s holds

The research-backed replacement for sit-ups — trains the six-pack without bending the spine.

  1. Lie on your back, one knee bent (foot flat), the other leg straight
  2. Slide both hands under the lower back to preserve the natural arch
  3. Brace, then lift head + shoulders a few inches — just clear the shoulder blades
  4. Hold 8–10s, don't tuck the chin or flatten the back; lower slow
