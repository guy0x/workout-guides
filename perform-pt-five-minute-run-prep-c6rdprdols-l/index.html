💡 How to follow: A concise pre-run sequence covering calf and soleus preparation, tibialis anterior work, force absorption, hip rotation, multiplanar lunges and jumps, and active stretching. The creator calls the complete sequence five minutes but does not prescribe universal sets or reps; individualize the dose and progress impact gradually.
1

Calf Raises + Soleus Stretch — Prepare the Lower Leg

Block 1
Controlled reps; individualize the volume

Perform a single-leg eccentric calf raise with a slow lowering phase, then stretch the calf and bias the soleus. Use support if balance or ankle control is limited.

  1. Lower slowly
  2. Reach a comfortable bottom stretch
  3. Keep the foot tripod stable
  4. Do not force range
2

Tibialis Work + Step-Downs — Rehearse Force Absorption

Block 2
Controlled reps; stop before form degrades

Work the tibialis anterior, then use step-downs and single-leg drops to practice absorbing force. Start from a low height and keep the landing quiet.

  1. Use a low step
  2. Track the knee over the foot
  3. Absorb softly
  4. Progress impact gradually
3

Hip Rotation — Open the Full Range

Block 3
Move through controlled internal and external rotation

Warm the hips through internal and external rotation and a comfortable full range. Keep the pelvis controlled rather than forcing the end position.

  1. Move slowly first
  2. Explore both directions
  3. Keep the trunk steady
  4. Stay below painful range
4

Lunges + Lateral Jump Prep — Cover All Planes

Block 4
Forward/backward and lateral patterns; low-volume quality work

Use forward and backward lunges, then add explosive movement, lateral lunges, and single-leg jumping. Running is repeated single-leg force production, so practice quiet, controlled contacts.

  1. Own the lunge positions
  2. Keep lateral steps stable
  3. Land quietly
  4. Reduce jump height if needed
5

Active Hamstring + Hip Stretch — Finish Ready to Run

Block 5
Active, comfortable range; no forced stretching

Finish with active stretching for the hamstrings, hips, and back. Keep the movement dynamic enough to prepare for running rather than fatigue the tissues.

  1. Stay active through the range
  2. Keep the back comfortable
  3. Avoid bouncing into end range
  4. Finish feeling more ready, not exhausted
