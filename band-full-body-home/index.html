Band Full-Body — Home Session

~40 min · bands + bosu only · no gym · holiday / closure fallback

This is the home replacement for the Day 5 Full-Body KB/DB Complex — used when the gym is closed or the hours don't fit. Everything runs on bands + a bosu ball.

Structure: 6 exercises, straight through, 90s rest between exercises. Do 3–4 solid rounds of the full block if you have time; 1 round still counts.

⚠️ Read the band-anchoring safety box before your first set.
01

Anywhere Reset

WARM-UP
1 round · ~5 min · no equipment
Full-body primer — warms the core, hips and spine before you load anything.
  1. Jumping Twists — 30s. Small hops, feet shoulder-width, swing the hips so knees twist opposite the shoulders. Stay tall, land soft.
  2. Cat–Cow (Pump Stretch) — 8 slow reps. Hands under shoulders, knees under hips. Exhale into a rounded cat, inhale into a tall cow. Move vertebrae by vertebrae.
  3. Cossack Squats — 6/side. Wide stance, toes slightly out. Sit into one side, other leg straight, push through the planted heel to stand.
  4. Standing Stomach Vacuum — 5 × 10s hold. Exhale all the air out while hollowing the belly in. Pull the navel toward the spine, breathe shallow while holding.
  5. Glute Bridge — 12 reps. Heels close to hips, drive through the heels, pause 1s at the top. Don't arch the lower back.
Why: Hip mobility and glute activation off the floor first means the squats and swings later are driven by hips, not by your lower back.
02

Band Goblet Squat

QUADS · CORE
4 × 10 reps
Replaces the kettlebell goblet squat — front-loaded resistance that teaches depth and core bracing.
  1. Stand on the middle of the band with feet shoulder-width, both handles looped up and held at your chest, elbows down.
  2. Sit between the hips, letting the elbows travel down inside the knees. Chest stays proud.
  3. Drive up through the heels, squeezing the glutes at the top.
  4. Slow the descent to 3 seconds — the eccentric is where the band gives you the most.
Setup: Feet wider apart on the band = more tension. If the band snaps you upright rather than resisting, step wider or double it up.
Why: Front-loaded squatting trains the quads and teaches the core to brace under load — same stimulus as the bell version, minus the hinge coordination.
03

Band Bent-Over Row

BACK · PULL
4 × 10 reps
The main pulling movement of the session — treat it as the priority lift.
  1. Stand on the band with both feet, hinge forward until the torso is ~45°, back flat, core braced.
  2. Pull both handles to your hips/belly button, driving the elbows back tight against the ribcage.
  3. Squeeze the shoulder blades together for 1 second at the top, then lower under control.
  4. Alternate: anchor the band in a door hinge at chest height and row from a standing position for a different angle.
Setup: Shorter band / wider stance = more resistance. Set it so the hardest point is at the squeeze, not the start.
Why: Horizontal pulling balances all the pressing in the program and builds the mid-back that holds your posture. This is the exercise bands replace worst — so give it the most attention.
04

Band Swing

HIPS · POSTERIOR
4 × 12 reps
The hinge power move — the band's variable resistance actually suits a swing well.
  1. Stand on the band with both feet, handles held in front of the hips, arms relaxed.
  2. Hinge at the hips — not a squat. Push the hips back, soft knees, spine neutral. The band goes taut behind you.
  3. Snap the hips forward explosively. The arms are ropes — they just guide the handles up. Do not lift with the arms.
  4. Brace the abs hard and squeeze the glutes at the top. Let it return, hinge again.
Setup: The band's tension rises with stretch, so the load peaks exactly when your hips snap — this is why swings work better than most moves on bands.
Why: Trains explosive hip extension — the highest-leverage pattern for the posterior chain. Also the best conditioning hit in this session.
05

Band Rotational High Pull

BACK · ROTATIONAL
3 × 10 reps / side
Rotation plus pull — explosive torso power and the oblique/back connection.
  1. Stand on the band with one foot, gripping the handle with the opposite hand.
  2. Drive the hips first, then pull high and rotate — lead with the elbow, not the hand.
  3. Finish with the hand around shoulder height, torso rotated through.
  4. Control the return slowly. Complete all reps on one side before switching.
Setup: Anchor or stand on the band on the same side as the working hand's opposite foot for the cleanest rotational line.
Why: Rotational pulling is the most under-trained pattern in most programs — it connects the hips to the upper back, and protects the lower back when you rotate under load.
06

Band Thruster

SHOULDERS · LEGS
3 × 8 reps
Full-body squat-to-press — the one move in this session that taxes everything at once.
  1. Stand on the band, handles racked at shoulder height, palms forward.
  2. Squat deep, chest proud, sitting back into the hips.
  3. Drive up explosively through the legs and press the handles overhead in one continuous motion — the legs launch the press.
  4. Lower the handles back to the rack position as you descend into the next squat. Keep the core braced the whole time.
Setup: If the overhead press is hard at the top, take some tension off by narrowing your stance on the band — the squat is the point, the press rides along.
Why: A thruster links leg drive to overhead pressing — it's the closest this band session gets to a true whole-body complex, and it's where your heart rate spikes.
07

Bosu Plank Rollout

CORE FINISHER
3 × 10 reps
The ab-wheel replacement. One of the highest-tension core movements available with no plates.
  1. Kneel in front of the bosu with your forearms on the dome, body in a straight line from knees to head.
  2. Roll the dome forward from the core, not by pushing with the shoulders. Keep the lower back flat — do not let it sag into hyperextension.
  3. Go only as far as you can hold the flat back. That's your range for today.
  4. Pull back with the abs, not the arms. 3-second return.
🚨 If you feel it in your lower back rather than your abs, you've gone too far. Shorten the range immediately. This is the single most common way this exercise goes wrong.
Why: Anti-extension demand — the core's real job is stopping your spine from bending backwards under load. This trains exactly that, and it's the reason the ab wheel is in the original Day 5.

Band Anchoring — Safety

MANDATORY
🚨 Never anchor to a door handle on the swing side. Handles slip, and a slipping anchor turns the band into a whip aimed at your face. Anchor to a door hinge, a heavy furniture leg, or a purpose-built anchor point.
🚨 Inspect the band for nicks and tears before every set. A snapped band under tension is the only genuine injury risk in this whole session. If any band shows damage, retire it.
✅ Peak resistance should land at the TOP of each movement, not the start. If the hardest part is the beginning, shorten the band or step wider.
✅ Never anchor at eye level. Keep anchors below chest height so a failure releases away from your face.

How To Run It

PROTOCOL
✅ Structure: exercises 02 → 07 straight through. 90s rest between exercises. 3–4 rounds of the block for a full session; 1–2 rounds is a legitimate short version.
✅ Warm-up is not optional — the Anywhere Reset primes the hips and core that the squats and swings depend on.
✅ Progression: when you hit the top of the rep range on every set with clean form, shorten the band (grip lower on it) or double it up. Don't chase reps past form.
🚨 Skip this session entirely on Yom Kippur (Sep 21) and on Erev Yom Kippur (Sep 20) — going into a 25-hour dry fast, preserving glycogen and fluid beats any workout.
✅ Knee note: this session loads the knees through the squats, swings and thrusters. If the joint is unhappy, swap the whole session for the Knee Morning PT routine instead — it needs no equipment and is the higher-priority work.
