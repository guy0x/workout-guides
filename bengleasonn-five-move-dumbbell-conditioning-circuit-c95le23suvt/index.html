💡 How to follow: A compact five-movement conditioning sequence using dumbbells and bodyweight: man makers, lunge to press, curl to squat, squat-ski, and plank pull-across. The source supplies exercise labels and rep counts but does not specify rounds, rest, or load; treat the listed doses as the source prescription and choose a load that preserves control.
1

Man Makers — Start the Full-Body Sequence

Move 1
8 each side

The source opens with a man-maker pattern combining a plank position and a dumbbell-driven full-body transition. Use the stated eight reps each side, but reduce the load or step the feet rather than losing trunk position.

  1. Brace before moving the dumbbells
  2. Keep the shoulders organized over the hands
  3. Step rather than jump when control is limited
  4. Stop if the low back sags
2

Lunge to Press — Link Legs and Shoulders

Move 2
10 reps

Step into a lunge and finish with an overhead press. Keep the front foot stable and press only through a range that leaves the ribs and pelvis controlled.

  1. Make the lunge base stable
  2. Track the front knee with the foot
  3. Stand tall before pressing
  4. Use a lighter load if the ribs flare
3

Curl to Squat — Coordinate the Transition

Move 3
12 reps

Combine a dumbbell curl with a squat. Treat the curl and squat as one controlled pattern rather than rushing the arm action while descending.

  1. Keep the elbows close during the curl
  2. Sit into a controlled squat
  3. Keep the feet planted
  4. Stand fully without leaning back
4

Squat Ski — Stay Rhythmic Under Load

Move 4
12 reps

The squat-ski pattern adds a rhythmic conditioning element to the circuit. Keep the squat shallow enough to maintain balance and let the arms follow the lower-body rhythm without swinging the weights aggressively.

  1. Keep the feet grounded
  2. Stay tall through the chest
  3. Use a controlled range
  4. Slow down before the weights pull you off line
5

Plank Pull Across — Finish with Trunk Control

Move 5
12 each side

From a plank, pull the dumbbell across the body while resisting rotation. Widen the feet or perform the movement from the knees as a regression; the source does not prescribe rounds or rest.

  1. Set the plank before pulling
  2. Keep the hips as level as possible
  3. Drag the dumbbell rather than jerking it
  4. Place the knees down if the trunk rotates
