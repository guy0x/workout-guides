💡 How to follow: A chair-assisted core routine with a straight-leg version, a bent-leg variation, and a floor-based hip-lift/knee-lowering movement. The source prescribes ten repetitions for each exercise for three rounds; its lower-belly and anterior-pelvic-tilt framing is preserved as an unverified creator claim, not a medical promise.
1

Chair-Assisted Straight-Leg Core — Keep the Back Long

Drill 1
10 reps · 3 rounds

Place the balls of the feet on a stable chair edge and the hands on the mat. With the back straight and shoulders supported, exhale to straighten the legs and inhale to bring them back.

  1. Secure the chair before starting
  2. Keep the shoulders from collapsing
  3. Maintain a long back
  4. Move only through a controlled range
2

Chair-Assisted Bent-Leg Variation — Control the Knee Tuck

Drill 2
10 reps · 3 rounds

Move the arms slightly farther forward and keep the legs bent instead of fully straightening them. Follow the source's exhale-up, inhale-down rhythm without losing shoulder or trunk control.

  1. Keep the knees bent as shown
  2. Do not let the shoulders collapse
  3. Exhale through the effort
  4. Use a smaller range if the back shifts
3

Hip Lift + Knee Lower — Finish with Controlled Range

Drill 3
10 reps · 3 rounds

From the floor-based setup, lift the hips and bring the knees downward as low as controlled before returning. Keep the belly engaged and do not force the depth promised by the creator's aesthetic framing.

  1. Lift without arching aggressively
  2. Lower the knees only as far as control allows
  3. Keep the belly gently braced
  4. Stop if the back or hips become painful
