💡 How to follow: A four-part spinal-mobility sequence: rotate, add gentle extension, progress to a supported wall bridge, then finish with shoulder rolls. The creator recommends moving slowly for roughly 45–60 seconds per drill and exploring only the range you can control comfortably.
1

Wall Rotations — Mobilize the Thoracic Spine

Drill 1
45–60 sec · slow continuous reps

Rotate the torso in a controlled range while using the wall as a positional reference. Keep the movement exploratory rather than forcing the shoulder or ribs toward the wall.

  1. Stand close enough to the wall to use it for orientation, not leverage.
  2. Rotate from the upper back while keeping the pelvis relatively quiet.
  3. Breathe continuously and move slowly through a comfortable range.
2

Wall Rotation & Arch — Add Gentle Extension

Drill 2
45–60 sec · slow reps

Combine thoracic rotation with a small amount of spinal extension. Treat the arch as an addition to the rotation, not a deep backbend.

  1. Rotate first, then add only the extension you can control.
  2. Keep the ribs from flaring aggressively as you reach back.
  3. Stop short of pinching in the low back or shoulder.
3

Full Wall Bridge — Supported Spinal Extension

Drill 3
45–60 sec · shallow, controlled reps

Use the wall for support while moving into a deeper extension pattern. This is the most demanding progression in the reel; regress by reducing the angle and range.

  1. Place the hands securely and keep the feet stable before leaning back.
  2. Press through the hands without collapsing into the shoulders.
  3. Use a shallow angle if the wrists, shoulders, or back feel restricted.
4

Shoulder Rolls — Finish With Fluid Upper-Back Motion

Drill 4
45–60 sec · relaxed circles

Finish with gentle shoulder rolls and fluid upper-spine movement. Keep the neck relaxed and let the shoulders move without shrugging forcefully.

  1. Stand in a comfortable stance with soft knees.
  2. Circle the shoulders smoothly without forcing the neck.
  3. Finish with easy breathing and no residual pain.

Range-of-motion rules

Slow beats deep. Use the creator's 45–60-second dose as a reference, but stay inside a controlled, comfortable range.

Regress the bridge. The wall bridge is optional and should be made shallower if the wrists, shoulders, or back cannot tolerate the position.

Pain is a stop signal. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness, or radiating symptoms; this guide is movement reference, not diagnosis.
