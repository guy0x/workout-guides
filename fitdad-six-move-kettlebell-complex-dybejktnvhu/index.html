Six-Move Kettlebell Complex — Full-Body Strength + Power

💡 How to follow: A six-movement single-kettlebell complex combining rows, swings, rotational high pulls, goblet squats, jump lunges, and kettlebell push-ups. The source prescribes five reps of each movement for six total rounds with 1–2 minutes rest; start lighter than ego suggests.
1

Two-Hand Bent-Over Row — Brace and Pull

Move 1
5 reps

Hinge at the hips and row the kettlebell toward the chest with both hands on the horn. Keep the trunk braced instead of rounding through the lower back.

  1. Set a stable hip hinge
  2. Brace the core
  3. Pull the horn toward the chest
  4. Lower with control
2

Kettlebell Swing — Snap the Hips

Move 2
5 reps

Use a powerful hip hinge to project the kettlebell forward. The hips generate the swing; the arms guide the bell rather than lifting it.

  1. Hike the bell back safely
  2. Snap the hips
  3. Keep the spine long
  4. Do not lift with the arms
3

Single-Arm Rotational High Pull — Drive and Rotate

Move 3
5 reps each side

Drive through the hips and pull the kettlebell high with one arm while allowing the body to rotate naturally. Keep the shoulder organized as fatigue builds.

  1. Drive from the hips
  2. Pull high without yanking
  3. Rotate naturally
  4. Control the return
4

Goblet Squat — Stay Tall and Sit Deep

Move 4
5 reps

Hold the kettlebell by the horn at the chest and squat with a tall trunk. Descend to a depth you can control, then drive through the heels.

  1. Rack the bell at the chest
  2. Stay tall
  3. Sit between the hips
  4. Drive through the heels
5

Alternating Jump Lunge + Bell Rotation — Control Fatigue

Move 5
5 alternating reps

Alternate the lunge stance while rotating the kettlebell side to side. This is the complex's high-impact coordination block; step back to a non-jumping lunge if needed.

  1. Land softly
  2. Keep the front knee controlled
  3. Rotate without losing balance
  4. Use a step-back regression when needed
6

Kettlebell Push-Up — Finish the Round

Move 6
5 reps

Finish the complex with kettlebell push-ups, maintaining a rigid plank line and controlled depth. Set the bell securely before loading it with the hands.

  1. Set the bell on a stable base
  2. Brace the plank
  3. Keep the elbows controlled
  4. Stop before form breaks
