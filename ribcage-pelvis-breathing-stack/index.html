Stacked Ribcage–Pelvis Position

Assessment
Understand the position before moving

The creator frames the problem as pressure pushing the organs down and forward. The proposed long-term direction is a stacked ribcage, pelvis, and head with room to breathe into the back of the ribcage.

  1. Keep the ribcage and pelvis stacked
  2. Avoid forcing the chest up
  3. Create space to breathe into the back of the ribcage
2

Supported Ball Sit + Pelvic-Floor Release

Drill 1
Slow breathing; no fixed rep prescription

Sit on a ball with the knees above the hips and the feet close enough to avoid rounding and sliding backward. Relax the pelvic floor downward and feel the inhale expand from the base of the ball toward the chest.

  1. Spread the sit bones gently
  2. Keep knees above hips
  3. Do not place the feet so far forward that the back rounds
  4. Relax the pelvic floor downward
  5. Breathe from the ball up into the chest
3

Basketball Ribcage Repositioning

Drill 2
Slow inhale and slow exhale

Place a basketball under the sternum on the floor, let the legs fall forward, and breathe against the ball to encourage the ribcage backward in space. Use the supported hand triangle and keep the head looking toward the thumbs without overextending or flexing the neck.

  1. Place the ball under the sternum
  2. Let the legs fall forward
  3. Use the hands in a grounded triangle
  4. Look toward the thumbs
  5. Take a slow inhale and slow exhale
  6. Avoid neck overextension
