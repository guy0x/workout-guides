💡 How to follow: Two grip corrections for kettlebell work: hold the handle from the corner rather than the middle, and point the knuckles toward the ceiling when the bell is overhead. The reel presents these as technique and injury-prevention cues, not individualized medical advice.
1

Corner Grip — Not the Middle of the Handle

Setup
Use for swings, cleans, and overhead work

The creator contrasts a middle-of-handle grip with holding the kettlebell from the corner. The corner position is presented as the more stable and efficient setup through the bell's arc.

  1. Do not default to the middle of the handle
  2. Set the hand at the corner of the handle
  3. Keep the grip stable through swings and cleans
2

Knuckles to the Ceiling Overhead

Overhead Position
Use whenever the kettlebell is overhead

For overhead kettlebell movements, turn the wrist so the knuckles point directly toward the ceiling. The creator warns that a bent-back wrist can contribute to poor loading and excessive backward lean.

  1. Turn the wrist into the corrected position
  2. Point the knuckles directly at the ceiling
  3. Avoid leaning too far back
  4. Keep the bell stacked over the body
3

Apply the Cue to Snatch, Press, or Get-Up

Transfer
Rehearse the position before loading

The same overhead wrist cue applies to a snatch, press, or get-up. Practice the hand and wrist position with a load you can control before progressing; the reel frames this as protection for the wrist, shoulder, and back.

  1. Keep knuckles pointed upward
  2. Use the cue in snatches, presses, and get-ups
  3. Choose a controllable load
  4. Stop if pain appears
