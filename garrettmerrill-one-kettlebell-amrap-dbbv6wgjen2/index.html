One-Kettlebell AMRAP — Five-Move Full-Body Circuit

💡 How to follow: A five-movement single-kettlebell circuit demonstrated as a 20-minute AMRAP: ten reps per movement, repeated for as many controlled rounds as possible. Keep the bell light enough to preserve technique through halos, rotation, overhead work, and the over-the-shoulder pullover.
1

Seated Halo Press-Out — Prime the Shoulders

Move 1
10 reps

From a seated or kneeling setup, circle the kettlebell around the head and press it out under control. Keep the ribs organized as the bell moves overhead and forward.

  1. Use a light bell
  2. Keep the ribs controlled
  3. Circle close to the head
  4. Press out without shrugging
2

Cross-Body Lunge — Load the Opposite Pattern

Move 2
10 reps

Step into the lunge while swinging the kettlebell across the leg that bends. Keep the foot, knee, and trunk coordinated through the descent.

  1. Step into a stable base
  2. Swing across the bending leg
  3. Keep the front knee controlled
  4. Drive back up with balance
3

Halo into Thruster — Squat and Press

Move 3
10 reps

Complete a halo around the head, descend into a squat, and drive the kettlebell overhead. Use a controlled squat depth and do not rush the transition between the halo and press.

  1. Keep the halo controlled
  2. Sit into the squat
  3. Stay tall through the trunk
  4. Drive overhead from the legs
4

Rotational Thruster — Coordinate the Turn

Move 4
10 reps

Combine a squat and press with a rotational pattern. Keep the rotation deliberate so the trunk, hips, and shoulder move together rather than twisting under load.

  1. Rotate from the hips and trunk
  2. Keep the footwork stable
  3. Press only through a controlled range
  4. Use a lighter bell if balance slips
5

Over-the-Shoulder Pullover — Finish the Round

Move 5
10 reps / controlled continuous work

Move the kettlebell over one shoulder, then the other, before bringing it overhead to complete the repetition. Keep the shoulder position and trunk control ahead of speed.

  1. Keep the bell close
  2. Move over one shoulder at a time
  3. Control the return overhead
  4. Stop before the shoulder loses position
