💡 How to follow: Check your gait first: when the foot leaves the ground, does the heel turn IN toward the body (compression) or AWAY (healthy release)? If yours turns in, do the toe tucks daily to straighten the feet, restore toe mobility and open the ankles. Prop the knees if the stretch is too much.
1

Gait Check — Heel Turns In vs Away

Assessment
Watch your own walking

Diagnostic: when the foot leaves the ground, a heel that turns IN toward the body signals compression through the hip, foot and ankle. A heel that turns AWAY (straight feet, narrow stance) is the correct way for the body to release pressure — and looks more fluid.

  1. Film or watch yourself walking from behind
  2. Note which way the heel turns as the foot leaves the ground
  3. Heel in = compression — fix with toe tucks below
  4. Heel away + straight feet = healthy gait pattern
2

Toe Tucks

Drill
Daily, hold each rep

The fix: tuck the toes under to straighten the feet, restore toe mobility and open the ankle. Keep the feet straight — do not let the heels fall out to the side or you lose the big-toe-pad connection.

  1. Kneel with toes tucked under (top of foot on the floor)
  2. Sit back gently to add stretch — feet stay straight
  3. Keep the big-toe pad connected to the floor
  4. Hold and breathe; repeat several times
  5. Do daily to straighten feet and open the ankles
3

Regression — Prop the Knees

Modification
As needed

If the toe-tuck stretch is too much pressure at the foot/ankle, or you cannot get into it, prop the knees up with a towel or yoga blocks to take pressure off — and keep the feet straight.

  1. Place a rolled towel or yoga blocks under the knees
  2. Let the props carry some of the bodyweight
  3. Keep the feet straight — heels not falling sideways
  4. Maintain the big-toe connection for the stretch
4

Finish Cue — Straight Feet, Big-Toe Connection

Form
Throughout

The final cue: keep the feet straight and the big-toe pad down. If the heels fall off to the side you have lost the connection needed for a good stretch — reset and re-tuck.

  1. Feet straight — no heel roll-out
  2. Big-toe pad stays connected to the floor
  3. If connection is lost, reset and re-tuck
  4. Aim for fluid, pressure-releasing movement
