💡 How to follow: 💡 How to follow: Antoine Marneffe (@antoine.calisthenics) makes the point in one reel — everyone wants the front lever, the planche and the L-sit, but what actually builds a bulletproof core is the boring stuff: hang, raise the knees, hold the body straight, lift the legs off the floor. Cards 1–4 are the basics; card 5 is what they turn into. Bar + low ledge + floor, no other equipment.
1

Dead Hang → Active Hang

Grip · Shoulders · Base
Not specified in reel

Everything starts from the hang. A dead hang decompresses the spine and builds the grip and shoulder stability every later hold is built on — then you make it an active hang by pulling the shoulder blades down and back.

  1. Grip the bar just outside shoulder width, overhand, thumbs wrapped
  2. Hang with arms straight and feet clear of the ground — let the body weight stretch you long
  3. Dead hang: shoulders relaxed up by the ears; Active hang: pull the shoulder blades down and back, ribs stacked over the pelvis
  4. Legs together, toes pointed, no swinging — breathe
  5. Build to 3 sets of 30–45 s. Use the active hang as your working position for everything below
2

Hanging Knee Raise

Lower Abs · Control
Not specified in reel

The reel's real core builder: knees to the chest while hanging — the movement you can already do before any lever. It trains the abs to lift the pelvis, not just crunch the spine.

  1. Start in an active hang — shoulders down, ribs down, no forward swing
  2. Curl the pelvis up and bring the knees toward the chest; knees finish above hip height
  3. Do NOT swing or kip — if the body travels, you are pushing the bar away, not lifting your knees
  4. Lower slowly (2–3 s down) and stop the swing dead at the bottom before the next rep
  5. 8–12 controlled reps × 3. When 12 clean reps is easy, straighten the legs into a straight-leg raise
3

Ledge Body Lever — Straight-Body Hold

Anti-Extension · Full Body
Not specified in reel

The strongest core drill in the reel, done on nothing but a low ledge: the whole body held in one straight line with only the upper back on the ledge. This is the exact strength behind a dragon flag.

  1. Lie on your back on a low ledge or bench, hands gripping behind your head, upper back and head supported
  2. Lift the hips and legs off the ledge — one straight line from shoulders through hips to toes
  3. Squeeze the glutes and brace the abs so the hips never sag; the lower back stays flat, never arched
  4. Only the upper back stays in contact with the ledge — the rest of the body is floating
  5. 5–15 s hold × 3, or slow 5-second negatives from the top. Add time before you add leverage
4

Seated Leg Raise — Floor L-Sit Prep

Hip Flexors · Abs
Not specified in reel

No bar, no ledge, just floor: sit, lean back on the hands and lift both straight legs. This is the entry point to the L-sit and the V-sit — and the reason the sand version in the reel looks easy.

  1. Sit with the legs straight in front, hands planted slightly behind the hips, fingers pointing away
  2. Lean back ~30–45°, press the hands down into the floor and lift both legs off the ground
  3. Keep the knees straight and the legs together — the lift comes from the hips, not from swinging
  4. Chest stays tall; if the lower back rounds or the legs drop, tuck the knees slightly and rebuild
  5. 8–12 slow reps or a 10–20 s hold × 3. Feet the ground between reps — quality over slop
5

Where This Leads — Front Lever, L-Sit, Planche

The Goal, Not The Method
Not specified in reel

This half of the reel is what everyone *thinks* built the core. These holds are the result of the four basics above — they are the same positions with longer levers and harder leverage.

  1. Active hang → tuck front lever → advanced tuck → full front lever
  2. Ledge body lever → dragon flag (straight body, only the shoulders on the bench)
  3. Floor L-sit → hanging L-sit → V-sit
  4. Progress only when the current version is a clean 3 × 15 s with zero sag
  5. The line from the reel: nothing here replaces the basics — the basics ARE the training
